# Ticket history

Read this when a product requirement, business constraint, or issue discussion may explain the design. Linear is one example; use the actual configured tracker and sufficient supported tools.

Start with linked ticket IDs. Read the full issue, relevant comments, parent or duplicate relationships, and attached project documents. Search the feature name, real symbol, domain terms, and relevant period if direct links are missing.

Parents can carry the product reason while a child describes only the implementation. Labels, milestones, and deadlines are leads; a label alone does not establish why someone chose this code.

## Check the timeline

Compare the issue's scope and dates with the target's change. A closed-and-reopened issue, duplicate chain, or revised requirement may describe another version of the plan.

Read a filled motivation section for concrete facts. Generic template text is weak evidence. Access-restricted issues are a gap, not a reason to guess.

Return ticket ID, title, URL, relevant short quotation or faithful paraphrase, author/date, parent or project, and its fit to the target. Preserve an original human ruling when its exact scope matters.
